Cleanuparr, Decluttarr, plungarr

All three watch the arr queues and remove downloads that are going nowhere. The difference is what happens to a download that could still be imported, and which download client the tool was built around. Reviewed against both projects' documentation and source in September 2026; corrections welcome as issues.

The one-line difference

Cleanuparr and Decluttarr are queue cleaners: they decide a download is bad and remove it. plungarr also finishes the import when the only problem was a technicality, distinguishes the reasons a download is stuck and applies a policy per reason, and was built around SABnzbd.

What plungarr does that they do not

Where they are ahead

  • Torrents. Private-tracker handling, protected tags, seeding cleanup, “downloading metadata” and slow-speed strikes. Cleanuparr integrates qBittorrent, Deluge, Transmission, µTorrent and rTorrent; Decluttarr integrates qBittorrent. plungarr has no torrent-client integration; its import handling still works for torrent users because it operates on the arr side.
  • More arrs. Both support Lidarr, Readarr and Whisparr; Cleanuparr adds Sportarr and LazyLibrarian. plungarr supports Sonarr and Radarr.
  • Cleanuparr has a web UI, a community blocklist of known-bad files, missing-content and unmet-cutoff searching, and more notification targets including Notifiarr, Telegram and Pushover.
  • Decluttarr removes orphaned, unmonitored and missing-file downloads and can detect deletions in the media folders.

Where all three agree

  • Multiple arr instances in one container.
  • A dry-run or test mode.
  • Remove, blocklist and search again as the default outcome for a bad download. Their strike counters and plungarr's two-sighting age gate are the same idea.

Which to use

Torrent-first, or you want buttons: Cleanuparr. Usenet-first, or your pile is mostly “manual import required” rows that only needed finishing: plungarr. They do not conflict, but do not point two tools at the same queue with overlapping rules.
